## 2. Do you have any experience or tips for using AI?

Through hands-on experience using AI, I have learned several core principles and tips to maximize its potential without losing independent thinking:

### Tip 1: Precise and context-rich prompt engineering
* **The C-R-I-O framework:** When giving tasks to AI, provide four elements:
  * **C (Context):** Who you are, what problem you are solving, and what language/technology you are using.
  * **R (Role):** Give the AI a role (e.g., *"Act as a veteran software engineer specializing in C++"*).
  * **I (Instruction):** Provide concrete instructions, clearly stating what to do and what not to do.
  * **O (Output format):** Specify the desired format (tables, Markdown, or concise bullet points).
* **Break down problems:** Avoid vague, monolithic requests. Instead, break problems into logical steps and prompt the AI iteratively (Chain-of-thought prompting).

### Tip 2: "Verify, Don't Trust Blindly"
* AI always carries the risk of **"hallucination"**—generating inaccurate information, non-existent libraries, or made-up functions convincingly.
* **Mandatory rule:** Always cross-check AI answers with official documentation, and manually run and verify every line of code before using it in a project.

### Tip 3: Treat AI as a "Co-pilot", not a "Replacement"
* Do not use AI to do all the coursework without understanding the core concepts.
* Use AI to stimulate your thinking. For instance, instead of asking *"Solve this problem for me"*, ask *"Give me 3 approaches to optimize this problem"*, or *"Why is my code running with $O(n^2)$ complexity instead of $O(n)$?"*.
* You must be the one who thoroughly understands the final solution to confidently present and answer questions from instructors.

### Tip 4: Data privacy & academic integrity
* **Never share sensitive data:** Avoid entering personal private information, passwords, API keys, or proprietary source code onto public AI platforms.
* **Transparency & accountability:** Always be honest and declare your AI usage (AI Usage Declaration) according to course policies, and take full responsibility for the accuracy and safety of the output.
